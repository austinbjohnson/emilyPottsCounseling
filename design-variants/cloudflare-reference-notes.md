# 1.1.1.1 reference: observed interaction language

Austin named https://1.1.1.1/ as a quality reference. In Chrome it redirected to https://one.one.one.one/. I inspected the rendered desktop page and a 390 × 844 phone viewport, scrolled through several sections, read the page structure and checked positioning/motion styles. This is inspiration for interaction quality, not a source of code, artwork, claims or branding for Emily.

## What the page actually does

- The desktop first screen reserves extensive white space for one colourful oversized `1.1.1.1` mark, one strong promise and conspicuous platform actions. A downward mark signals continuation. The mobile hero stacks those actions beneath the same mark and copy.
- Normal vertical scrolling reveals large, clearly separated chapters. Each chapter pairs a concise heading and a modest amount of explanatory text with an oversized product illustration. Large abstract colour fields enter and leave the viewport around those illustrations, so the page feels visually different after each ordinary scroll.
- The product illustrations and colour fields create stronger progression than UI widgets. The page does not ask visitors to choose a concern, manipulate a diagram or complete a sequence to understand its main action.
- On the observed page, a computed-style inspection of 503 body descendants found **no CSS animation names and no fixed or sticky elements**. The inspected phone scrolls behaved normally. This does not prove that every asset or browser state is motionless, but it rules out the scroll hijacking and sticky scene assumptions we previously made about the reference.
- The main action is repeated and specific. Desktop navigation is sparse; the phone header becomes simpler. Supporting material remains lower on the page or behind direct links.

## Translation for Emily

- Use one authored, recognizable visual idea at a generous scale, not three small illustrations inside cards. It can originate in verified portrait/nature material and custom code-native geometry, without pretending any photograph shows the current counselling room.
- Let colour and shape carry between short chapters so a normal phone scroll has a noticeable visual progression. This can work even when all required text and links remain static in the document and reduced-motion visitors see the same content.
- Keep the first screen economical: Emily Potts, verified RCC and location/online scope, direct Jane consultation, a clear cue that content continues. Put longer authored therapy explanations on About/Services/FAQs rather than copying them into a large homepage.
- Keep Jane reachable by an ordinary first-screen link and repeat it later in flow. Make Meet Emily, Services and fees, How I work, FAQs and Contact ordinary destinations, never rewards for completing an effect.
- Use gentle tonal contrast and predictable artwork instead of Cloudflare's saturated product palette. Strong scale and shape can provide distinction without flashing, parallax, autoplay audio, or difficult motion.

The reference's visible strength is **scale, pacing, illustration and CTA hierarchy**. The next concepts should be judged against that, with actual 320px Chrome interaction tests, rather than inventing complex controls solely to appear technically advanced.
