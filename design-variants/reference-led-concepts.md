# Reference-led mobile directions — pre-build review

Source: Austin pointed to https://1.1.1.1/ (redirects to https://one.one.one.one/) as the desired interaction-quality reference. See `cloudflare-reference-notes.md` for actual desktop and Chrome phone inspection. The page's observed strength came from scale, colour, large illustration, native scroll rhythm and direct actions. None of Cloudflare's assets, code or brand should be copied.

Earlier ten fanouts and four private interaction probes are preserved in `mobile-concept-review.md` and `revised-interaction-candidates.md`. They failed because the mobile result became cards, tabs, icons or a slight mask reveal. The next directions must give Emily's site a distinctive large-scale visual identity and useful page rhythm with substantially less homepage copy. The existing Jane action and detail routes remain direct. No invented therapeutic outcomes or counselling-room imagery.

## Astra synthesis 1 — Near / Across

An oversized, simplified but geographically accurate British Columbia illustration explains two actual service scales: in person in North Vancouver and online across BC. Emily's authentic, separately framed portrait introduces the page. At 320 × 568, identity, scope, Jane and Meet Emily occupy the first frame; the illustrated province enters at its foot. First normal scroll shows the full province, North Vancouver label and Services and fees; second reveals a short FAQs/Contact chapter and repeat Jane. One optional scroll-linked tint can fill the province as it enters view, representing already stated online availability. Labels, point, words, portrait and links remain still. Reduced/no-JS shows the completed graphic. **Risk:** a coverage checker or inaccurate map. Any map shape and North Vancouver placement require authoritative geographic source data, and the animation must add clarity rather than implying service spread or client locations.

## Astra synthesis 2 — Shoreline in Three Prints

Three large, source-derived illustrated compositions form a visual essay. The first pairs a separate Emily portrait with branch-and-water forms and direct Jane; the second changes to a pale-blue shoreline composition with a concise Ways to meet/Services link; the third uses a sparse lilac botanical silhouette around the free consultation, FAQs and Contact. A shoreline boundary visually continues across chapters, but nothing animates or intercepts scroll. At 320px each chapter should take roughly one phone frame and each useful route arrive within a normal scroll. Around 90–120 homepage words maximum, excluding essential footer. Reduced motion is identical. **Risk:** generic wellness blobs and decorative page length. Recognisable source-derived forms and distinct compositions are essential.

## Astra synthesis 3 — A person, then a place

A photographic editorial sequence: full-width small first-frame Jane and Emily's real portrait; at the first scroll, her portrait visibly crosses a white-to-pale-lake-blue chapter boundary and About appears; next scroll brings a full-bleed existing lakeshore photograph plus Therapy; a sharply bounded evergreen practical section follows with Services and fees, FAQs, Contact and repeat Jane. No parallax or animation. Portrait and shoreline remain separate photographs; no current room is implied. At 320 × 568, identity/location/Jane fit by y294 and portrait starts around y326. **Risk:** familiar therapist site with bigger photos. Composition must make the person-to-place scale change and practical conclusion feel deliberate, not like unrelated hero sections.

## Astra synthesis 4 — Alongside

Two original, broad, asymmetric code-native colour bands carry across the page, alongside Emily's authentic but separate portrait. First 320 × 568 frame: identity, brief counselling heading, verified North Vancouver/online BC, direct Jane by roughly y294, and the beginning of the two large bands/portrait below. On first scroll, the bands continue down opposite margins around a quiet white area with Meet Emily and an About link; a broad diagonal carries the art into the next chapter. Second scroll: that diagonal becomes a muted-blue field around a separately framed landscape image, before the bands leave opposite edges and a plain practical section presents Therapy, Services and fees, FAQs, Contact and Jane. No bands converge at booking, enclose the visitor/portrait, represent a client journey or cover text. Native scroll reveals static, reserved geometry; reduced motion is identical. **Risk:** generic wellness ribbon/path or visual crowding. Asymmetry, spacious content clearance and human portrait must make it credible.

## Shared non-negotiables

All concepts keep a clearly labelled free consultation link to Emily's verified Jane destination near the top, plus normal About/Therapy/Services/FAQs/Contact links and a later Jane action. Browser scrolling, focus and link targets remain native; no page progress demand. Existing public repo portrait/landscape assets are separate and must not pretend to depict a current session or share a location. Low sensory load, stable text, direct keyboard path, 200% zoom and a complete static/no-JS presentation are required. Build only concepts that survive Sol and Luna's independent constructive critique, then test 320/390px mobile and desktop in Chrome before considering a public preview update.

## Independent adversarial review before implementation

Sol and Luna each reviewed the four concepts against the observed Cloudflare page and the earlier failed probes. Both selected **Near / Across** and **Shoreline in Three Prints** for bounded local prototypes. They rejected **A person, then a place** as an ordinary therapist photography sequence and **Alongside** as broad but arbitrary wellness ribbons. Those rejected directions remain documented, not built.

Both reviewers removed Near / Across's proposed scroll tint. The finished geographic illustration must itself explain two truthful facts: a single North Vancouver in-person point and a separate online-across-BC label. No coverage rings, spreading fields, extra points or location input. Its shape and point need a credible geographic source. The practical Services and fees route must arrive with the map on the first normal phone scroll, not after a large decorative gap. The portrait stays a separately framed photograph.

For Shoreline, the reviewers limited the art to one source-derived, recognisable branch/water/shoreline vocabulary. They removed the unrelated botanical third style. Three phone frames must change clearly in scale, silhouette and empty space: human portrait, broad shoreline, sparse practical close. Direct Jane and ordinary detail links are available in the first frame; artwork cannot make the user finish a visual sequence before reaching fees, FAQs or Contact. If the illustrations read as generic blobs or the useful links are delayed, reject the build rather than adding movement to disguise it.

The reviewed interpretation of Austin's reference is a **noticeable visual change during native scrolling**, created with oversized, carefully paced static compositions. The observed reference does not support claims of scroll hijacking or elaborate scroll animation. Both prototypes must work with no JavaScript and reduced motion; any later visual effect needs a separate information or usability reason.

## Local prototype verdict after Chrome review

**Near / Across: publish as the bounded reference-led preview.** The first 320px frame now shows identity, professional scope, direct Jane booking, About/Services/FAQs/Contact routes, Emily's portrait and the beginning of the actual BC silhouette. A normal roughly 250px scroll puts the province, North Vancouver marker, in-person/online labels and Services link together. The map is informative and visually specific to the service; source geometry and city marker provenance are documented with the prototype. It still merits visitor feedback about whether the simple map is immediately understood.

**Shoreline in Three Prints: retain locally, do not publish yet.** The revised branch/shore illustration and three colour chapters are clear and accessible at phone size. Chrome review and the prototype author's own critique found that the opening and ending still feel like a familiar therapist landing page. Publishing it as another supposedly distinctive option would repeat the prior problem. Its private source and before-code design plan remain for a later art-direction iteration.
